# Pure from-scratch self-play in Gen 1 random battles — the chapter, written down

Self-contained on purpose: a reader should need no other file and no prior conversation.
This is the successor to `DESIGN.md`, which was the roadmap and is deleted once its
content has migrated. `STATUS.md` is current state; `SESSION_LOGS.md` is the dated record
with every number and every correction; this file is the account.

Everything below is measured against poke-env 0.15.0 and a locally vendored Showdown
server, in `gen1randombattle`, battle phase only — no team building.

---

## 1. The claim

> **A PPO agent trained by pure from-scratch self-play, with the scripted benchmark held
> out of training entirely, reaches 0.6185 against `SimpleHeuristicsPlayer` in
> `gen1randombattle` — up from a 0.3996 plateau — clearing all four pre-registered
> milestones including parity with, and passage of, the scripted benchmark.**

The interesting word is **pure**, not the number. The published Pokémon-RL systems that
reach real strength are bootstrapped from human replays or distilled from a search
engine — train on expert games, then apply RL. This chapter deliberately does not do
that, and the point is to find out how far the unassisted lane goes.

### What "pure" means here, enforceably

A run is PURE iff the trained weights are a function only of (a) random initialization,
(b) experience generated by the agent playing against its own current or past weights,
and (c) the environment — observation, action space, reward. Excluded from any run
claiming purity:

- **No BC initialization.** Pointing `init_from` at any clone disqualifies the run.
- **No teacher or human data in training** — no Foul-Play tapes, no SH-clone data, no
  human replays, no distillation loss, no KL-to-BC anchor.
- **No scripted opponent in the training distribution.** SH, Foul Play and MaxBasePower
  never appear as training opponents. Opponents are the agent's own weights, current or
  pooled.
- **No reward channel derived from another agent's evaluation.**

Explicitly allowed, and worth stating because the line is where the claim can rot:
scripted agents as **eval anchors** (evaluation is not training — and holding SH out is
exactly what makes vs-SH admissible as a primary read); the **observation encoder**,
which is part of how the environment describes the state rather than a prior over how to
play it; and **design-time knowledge from published work** — hyperparameters,
architectures, reward designs. Those do not enter the weights. Using proven, unglamorous,
published technique inside this lane is not a compromise of the claim; smuggling a
*policy* into the observation would be.

**Disclosure, required by the purity rules themselves:** the network trunk was chosen by
a behaviour-cloning screen against a search teacher's banked tapes. No teacher data
entered any trained weight, but that is a real if weak design-time information channel
and pretending it is zero would be the sort of quiet claim this project has repeatedly
caught others making.

## 3. How it got there

Four rungs, cheapest falsifiable first, each pre-registered in its own config header
before launch.

**Rung 0 — throughput.** Engineering only. The loop was collection-bound; the server's
`simulator: 4` setting alone is worth +81% collection throughput.

**Rung 1 — signal: NULL.** The published gen-7 self-play system's five-term reward
shaping, ported and run on its own. It did not move the number. An earlier
team-preview/input arm had nulled too.

**Rung 2 — structure: 0.3996 → 0.5509, and this is the rung that did the work.** An
entity trunk (species/move embeddings, DeepSets team pooling) with a per-action scoring
head, replacing a flat MLP over a hand-built vector. Logits are formed as
`scorer([context ‖ entity])` rather than as a fixed-width output layer — the agent scores
*this move against this opponent* instead of learning slot-indexed preferences.
**Delta +0.1513, se_diff 0.0074, z = +20.5** — six times the credit line and ten times
2·se_diff. Every milestone through M3 was claimed here.

**The single most useful thing this chapter learned, and it is a negative-then-positive
sandwich:** reward shaping nulled, input enrichment nulled, and then **structure at
identical capacity** (626,059 parameters, under the same 681,994 ceiling) unlocked the
12M bootstrap. Handing the policy *"this action targets this entity"* beat making it
relearn that fact in every weight column. The lever that mattered was architectural, not
informational and not motivational.

**Rung 3 — scale, 12M → 50M.** +0.029, and the diagnostics said why it was small: value
explained-variance plateaus at 0.56–0.59 and the critic's effective rank collapses to
7–11 of 384. **A representation ceiling, not experience starvation** — which is why the
chapter then spent its remaining budget on representation levers rather than on more
steps.

**D25 — an auxiliary opponent-action head. 0.6185, the chapter's only credited lever.**
Gen 1 is simultaneous-move: both players commit before either resolves. The head predicts,
from the agent's own policy context, *which action the opponent is choosing on this turn* —
ground truth is free in self-play because the opponent is a frozen snapshot of the agent
itself. The head is train-time only and is not part of the evaluated agent; the
observation space is untouched.

**And it was controlled.** A second arm (D25-P) ran the identical head with **shuffled
labels** — same loss shape, same row count, same class marginals, same coefficient, same
cadence, zero information. It lands dead on the comparator (**0.5415** vs 0.54452, Δ
−0.0030) while the real head reaches 0.6185. Treatment-vs-placebo is **+0.0770** against a
2·se bar of 0.0347, so it credits. The information did the work, not the extra gradient.

**The licensed sentence is therefore "an explicit opponent-action model helps" — and never
"belief state".** The mechanism probe reads opponent entity features that the policy's own
scorer never receives, so it upper-bounds policy-usable belief rather than measuring it.

## 4. What did not work — part of the result, not an appendix

In a chapter about how far an unassisted lane goes, the failures map the lane.

**D18 — privileged (asymmetric) critic: NULL, falsifier fired.** During self-play the
critic's input was widened with the opponent seat's true hidden state (the actor's view
unchanged — the unbiased `V(h,s)` form, not privileged-only). Explained variance rose;
**win rate did not** (0.5364). The pre-stated falsifier said that exact pattern means the
critic fits information the policy cannot exploit — so the rung was killed rather than
tuned around.

**D19 — auxiliary opponent-TEAM prediction: KILLED at zero training lanes.** Predict the
species of the opponent's five hidden mons; the obvious "belief state" lever, imported
from games where hidden composition is correlated (DouZero+'s hidden hand). It died in
design, before any compute, and **the reason is a fact about the format**:

> Gen 1 randbats teams are *not* independent uniform draws — the generator is rejection
> sampling with a species clause and caps of 2 on shared type and shared weakness, and
> those caps bind on 0 of 600,000 sampled teams. But **88–90% of the resulting structure
> is a deterministic cap MASK** — a closed-form function of what has already been revealed
> — leaving a genuine belief residual of **0.024–0.034 nats against a 4.955-nat target**.
> At one revealed mon it is 0.034, and that is a ceiling.

So the head would have spent >90% of its loss fitting a constant. It was re-targeted to
opponent *action* prediction, which is the belief that actually bears on a
simultaneous-move decision, and that became D25. **A generalizable finding: an auxiliary
hidden-composition head cannot form a useful belief state in a format whose composition is
generated near-independently of play.** Full measurement, with positive controls proving
the estimator could see a signal if one existed, in `results/d19_closeout/`.

**D23 — regenerative L2-toward-init: letter-met, seed-fragile, NOT credited** (0.5897).
It cleared the +0.025 letter and failed the seed-clustered term. Recorded as a
non-credit rather than promoted, which is the discipline working.

**Screened out or confirmed dead elsewhere:** faint-differential reward shaping (−0.0004);
and, on independent external review, RND/curiosity, recurrence-first architectures,
periodic resets/ReDo, exotic optimizers, PopArt/symlog at bounded ±1 returns, and
reconstruction auxiliary losses. Exploitability was *measured* not to be the binding
constraint — a fresh best-response probe reached only 0.4765 against the frozen final,
with policy entropy healthy at 0.21–0.32 — which is why opponent-sampling work was never
run.

## 5. Honest scoping — what this is not

**This is not a strength record, and it does not enter the published field.**

Converting to human-ladder terms (source facts from the Metamon paper's Table 2 and
Fig 17; the conversion is ours): SH itself scores **39.7% GXE on Gen7RandomBattle and
41.2% on Gen9RandomBattle** — in random battles, with team-building removed, SH is
roughly twice the player it is in OU tiers. So **parity with SH is ~40% GXE, Glicko-1
≈ 1450–1500**, and a vs-SH number near the 0.489 parity mark means ~40% GXE, not "nearly
solved."

Where the field sits:

| system | format | GXE |
|---|---|---|
| poke-env SH — our benchmark | Gen7RB / Gen9RB | 39.7% / 41.2% |
| Huang & Lee 2019 — PPO self-play, no search | Gen7RB | 72% |
| ps-ppo — transformer PPO | Gen9RB | 76.7% |
| Wang 2024 — PPO + test-time MCTS | Gen4RB | 79.5% |
| Metamon SynRL-V2 — offline RL on human replays | **Gen1OU** | 79.9% |
| strong human players | — | 74–90% |

**The floor of the published pure-policy randbats field is 72%.** That gap is not a
shaping or step-count gap. Our 0.6185 is meaningfully past SH and still nowhere near
that floor — a purity-lane first, not a leaderboard result.

**Four caveats, all stated by the sources themselves, and they all cut the same way:**
n is tiny for SH's rows (56 and 60 battles, ±6.5pp); SH's low rating skews matchmaking
toward weak opponents, so its raw W–L is an **upper** bound; Fig 17's Glicko "is possibly
an overestimate"; and — the big one — **nobody has measured `gen1randombattle` on a human
ladder at all.** Every randbats row above is gen4/7/9 and every gen1 row is OU. This is a
cross-format extrapolation, not a measurement of our board. Note in particular that the
~80% Metamon figure is **Gen1OU — a different format from ours**, with team-building
present. Read all of it as scale-setting, not as a score.

The honest frame: the search engine this project distilled from scores 0.8307. Pure
self-play got to 0.6185 without ever seeing it, from a 0.3996 start. The gap is the result.

### Disclosures attached to the D25 claim

- **Transfer: TESTED, and it holds** (2026-08-16, zero lanes, `results/c4_transfer/`).
  This was the open one — the head is trained in mirror self-play, so the "opponent" it
  predicts is the agent's own frozen weights, and whether any of that transfers to a
  *different* opponent was untested. Pre-registered before the measurement, then run:
  take the same frozen reference checkpoint §5 used (in neither arm), have it play
  **`SimpleHeuristicsPlayer`** instead of itself, and ask whether the credited lanes'
  context decodes SH's next action better than the control lanes' does. **It does, at the
  minimum attainable p:** treatment **+0.0665** vs control **+0.0366** nats, complete
  separation of all five lanes against all five, exact permutation **p = 1/252 = 0.0040**
  against a 12/252 level — in both label spaces, on an opponent no lane ever trained
  against.

  **The obvious confound was found and cleared.** The credited lanes have ~1.7x more live
  context units (221–293 vs 131–169), and across the ten lanes live-unit count correlates
  with the statistic at **r = +0.94** — with *zero overlap* between arms, so "treatment"
  and "more live units" are perfectly confounded in the pre-registered design. (§5's
  mirror-tape specificity argument does **not** transfer here: there the same correlation
  was −0.453, the wrong sign to manufacture a positive.) A post-hoc capacity-matched
  control — every lane cut to exactly 131 randomly chosen live units, refit — leaves the
  gap intact: **+0.0318, p = 1/252 on all three draws**. Capacity is not what is doing the
  work.

  **What this licenses, and what it does not.** The representation transfers to an
  opponent outside the training distribution; the "it only models itself" reading is
  refuted. It does **not** discharge the self-model confound below — training is still
  mirror self-play — it does not license "belief state", and it does not make the head
  shipped: it remains train-time only.
- **The opponent model is still a self-model in training.** Mirror self-play is the
  regime; what the transfer probe shows is that the resulting representation is not
  *only* about itself.
- **Representational, not shipped.** The head is train-time only; nothing at evaluation
  time consults an opponent model. The claim is about what the auxiliary gradient did to
  the trunk.
- **Dose caveat — and the measured reason it cannot be closed with this control.** The
  placebo's auxiliary gradient reached only ~1.2–21.9% of the treatment's per-bin trunk
  dose, so "a *generic* auxiliary gradient of matched size would have helped too" is
  **untested**. It was going to be tested by re-running the placebo at a higher
  coefficient; a zero-lane design pass (2026-08-16) showed **that control cannot be
  built**, and the reason is worth more than the arm would have been:

  **A shuffled-label auxiliary head cannot be dosed, because it stops using the trunk.**
  The head has a `slot_bias` term whose documented purpose is to "absorb the class
  marginal at the optimum … the design against C3(a) (a head that fits P(class) and calls
  it a belief)" (`rl/networks/opp_action.py:126-128`). For a shuffled head, P(class) *is*
  the whole task — so it routes everything there and disconnects its input path.
  Measured on the banked lanes: placebo `|slot_bias|` grows 0.56 → 1.98 while its final
  scorer weight stays flat at 0.32 → 0.34; the treatment does the reverse (0.48 → 1.25,
  bias 0.09–0.29). The fraction of the auxiliary gradient that reaches the trunk therefore
  collapses from 0.44–0.54 at 1M to **0.05–0.09 at 12M**, against the treatment's
  0.51–0.62.

  That makes the mismatch **an identity, not a shortfall**: matching the trunk component
  requires ~58× the coefficient, which puts the *total* gradient at ~8.3× the treatment's,
  and the gap is exactly the ratio of the two trunk fractions. **No scalar changes a
  fraction** — not a constant coefficient and not a per-update controller. Two further
  walls confirm it independently: the auxiliary clip is applied *after* the coefficient
  (`rl/agents/ppo.py:796`), capping delivered dose at `clip × trunk-fraction` and putting
  bins 6–11 out of reach at **any** coefficient; and the head's own parameters are a
  separate Adam group fed only by the auxiliary loss (`:552`), so by scale-invariance the
  coefficient cannot change what the head learns — only how hard an input-ignoring head
  pushes.

  **So the honest status is not "untested" but "untested, and this control cannot test
  it."** Closing it needs an auxiliary task with permanent structure and zero opponent
  information — predicting the agent's *own* action, or regressing a fixed random
  projection of its own observation — which never exhausts its task, never disconnects,
  and holds its dose flat at a constant coefficient. Named, not run.

## 6. Method notes

**Locked eval protocol.** Final checkpoint, deterministic policy, ties as non-wins, vs
`SimpleHeuristicsPlayer`, 3000 battles/seed, 3 seeds pooled. Every arm from D23 onward
pooled 5×3000 — a disclosed deviation, conservative, not the locked number.

**Credit line.** A lever is credited iff pooled delta ≥ +0.025 **and** ≥ 2·se_diff, where
se_diff is the larger of the pooled-binomial and the seed-clustered se_diff. On this task
the clustered term always governs: between-lane seed spread runs 0.024–0.049, so the
operative bar sits ~0.03–0.05 above the comparator and a nominally large effect can still
fail. Three arms met the +0.025 letter and did not credit.

**Pre-registration.** Every arm's config header states its arms, zero-lane gates, primary
read with the credit line restated verbatim, secondaries, falsifiers, and the action on
each branch — written and ratified *before* launch. Headers were adversarially reviewed by
independent agents; that process repeatedly caught real defects before compute was spent,
including a parameter-ceiling breach, an optimizer-state bug that would have silently
handed auxiliary parameters the critic's Adam moments, and a mechanism read that would
have been null by construction.

**Evaluation honesty.** `eval/win_rate` comes from the environment's own outcome field,
never the sign of the return — a reward-sign inversion would otherwise report 100% and
pass its own detector. Showdown evaluations are unpaired and not reproducible; precision
is bought with battles, not with seeds or pairing machinery.

**Cost.** The whole chase, 78 training lanes, ran on CPU on one desktop: ~430 lane-hours
= 17.91 lane-days against a pre-declared abandon budget of 20.

## 8. Still open

- **Matched-dose control.** Attempted and found unbuildable — see §5's disclosure. The
  live alternative explanation for D25's gain now needs a *structured*
  zero-information auxiliary task, not a rescaled shuffled-label one.
- **Scale.** D25 credited at 12M and has never been carried to 50M. The 50M comparator's
  seed spread (sd 0.0756) puts the bar above the best single lane in repo history, so this
  needs a variance-reduction idea, not just compute.
- **The frontier.** 0.6185 against a search engine's 0.8307, in a lane that started at
  0.3996.
