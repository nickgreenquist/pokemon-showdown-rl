## 1. The one-line answer

The prior-work index's summary — *"36 poke-env state-tracking fixes (encoder-relevant ones upstreamed by 0.15.0)"* (`docs/prior_work/README.md:566-569`, literature-only) — is **mostly right but not fully right**. Twenty of Wang's changes are upstreamed or moot. **Four are still ABSENT in 0.15.0 and every one of them is gen4-live and gen1-dead**, i.e. our encoder has never had to care:

1. **Ability-set weather is not distinguished from move-set weather, and every `[upkeep]` line resets the weather's start turn** (`abstract_battle.py:755-761`).
2. **The sleep counter double-increments on every Sleep Talk turn** (`pokemon.py:482-483` reached twice per Sleep Talk, via `abstract_battle.py:726-741`). 25 of 295 gen4 randbats species carry `sleeptalk`.
3. **`maybe_trapped` is ignored by the action mask and by both baseline players** (`singles_env.py:236-240`, `battle.py:280-282`, `baselines.py:354`) — gen4 randbats has Arena Trap, Shadow Tag and Magnet Pull in the pool.
4. **No memory of a Pokémon's original item** once it is consumed or removed (`pokemon.py:1104-1112`, `end_item` at L405-406).

Plus two lower-stakes ABSENTs (`Move._revealed`; `TieBattleOrder` / `/offertie`) and one design divergence (the `_switch` de-duplication / request-driven switch-in pair) that 0.15.0 solves differently.

## 3. gen1 encoder assumptions this breaks

All four live ABSENTs are gen-2-or-later mechanics, which is exactly why our 828-dim gen1 encoder has never met them.

1. **"There is no weather."** Gen 1 has no weather at all, and SNAP `rl/envs/showdown.py` contains no `weather` reference (grep over the file: zero hits). `rl/envs/encoder_spec.py:56-61` already flags weather as a new global block for a gen-2+ spec. What this note adds: **the weather block cannot carry a turn counter derived from `battle.weather`'s dict value under poke-env 0.15.0** — the value is restamped every `[upkeep]`. Either encode presence-only, or compute the counter ourselves from `battle.turn` at the transition, or patch/subclass the `-weather` branch. (tree-verified + source-verified)
2. **"`status_counter` is a clean sleep clock."** `rl/envs/showdown.py:234` encodes `mon.status_counter / 16.0`. Gen 1 has no Sleep Talk (`sleeptalk` is a gen-2 move; PE's `gen1moves.json` has no `curse` and no `sleeptalk`), so the double-increment path never fires today. In gen4 it fires on 25/295 species' sets. `rl/envs/encoder_spec.py:63-65` already says "status COUNTER semantics also move (gen-2 sleep/toxic counters)" — this is a concrete instance with a named upstream bug, not just a semantics shift.
3. **"`trapped` is the whole trapping story."** `rl/envs/showdown.py:303` encodes `vec[4] = bool(battle.trapped)` and nothing else; gen 1's only trapping is partial-trap (Wrap/Fire Spin), handled at `showdown.py:306-317` as a move-list collapse. Gen 4 adds **ability**-based trapping with a *maybe* state that poke-env parses and then ignores. The gen4 spec needs a `maybe_trapped` feature AND a decision about the mask (see open questions).
4. **"Items and abilities don't exist."** `rl/envs/encoder_spec.py:53-55`: *"items and abilities: absent in gen 1, so there is no block for them."* This note sharpens the item requirement: poke-env 0.15.0 gives us `item` (current) but **not** `original_item`, and destroys the evidence at `end_item`. A gen4 item block that only reads `mon.item` will silently forget every consumed berry, every Knock Off victim and every Tricked Choice item.
5. **"A move in `mon.moves` is a move we've seen."** True in gen 1 without a set prior. `rl/envs/encoder_spec.py:69-71` flags `randbats_prior.py` as gen-1-only; if the gen4 prior populates `mon.moves` speculatively, we lose the seen/unseen distinction that Wang added `Move._revealed` for.

## 6. Open questions for the maintainer

1. **`maybe_trapped` and the action mask.** Three options: (a) leave 0.15.0's behaviour and eat the `[Unavailable choice]` retries plus the mask/legality mismatch; (b) mask switches out whenever `maybe_trapped` — safe, but forbids many *legal* switches (the flag fires on species that merely *could* have the trapping ability), which biases the policy toward staying in; (c) encode `maybe_trapped` as an observation feature, keep the mask permissive, and add an explicit retry-aware handler. **My recommendation: (c) + a bounded retry.** *The losing argument for (b):* it is the only option that makes the mask a true legality mask, which is our stated harness contract ("Action masking is a harness contract") — and a policy that never switches under a possible Arena Trap loses less than one that stalls the collector. I still think (c) wins because the false-positive rate is high and (b) permanently removes a legal action.
2. **Weather turn counter.** Do we (a) encode weather presence only, (b) track weather start-turn ourselves in the env wrapper, or (c) carry a small patched subclass of `AbstractBattle`'s `-weather` branch? (c) is the highest fidelity and the highest maintenance; (a) is the cheapest and loses gen4's "Sandstorm ends in 2 turns, don't switch the Rock in yet" read.
3. **Do we patch poke-env at all, or wrap it?** Wang forked. Our standing pins say exact versions in `pyproject.toml`; a fork means a vendored pin. A subclass-and-override wrapper inside `rl/envs/` keeps the pin and is testable in-tree. This decision governs #1, #2, the sleep counter and `original_item` at once.
4. **`/offertie` for gen4 collection.** gen4 randbats stall wars are long; our gen1 instrument is `/timer on` (binding, wire-verified). Is a `TieBattleOrder` equivalent worth adding for gen4, and if so does it corrupt the reward (a tie is a non-win under the locked protocol)?
5. **Sleep counter double-count.** Fix in our wrapper, or accept a 2× sleep clock on Rest/Sleep-Talk sets? Cheap to fix (suppress the SLP bump when the `moved` call came from an `overridden_move`), but it means touching the message parser.
